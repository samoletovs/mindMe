Why China for your family

The deciding factor — everyone gets in with no visa. Latvian citizens (you + the kids) have 30-day visa-free entry to China, valid through 31 Dec 2026. Your wife's Russian passport also gets 30-day visa-free entry under the Russia–China bilateral arrangement. No fees, no interviews, no waiting — a stark contrast to the US, where her passport would require a B1/B2 visa with months of uncertainty.
  • Cost-effective: the flights are the only big expense; food, trains and hotels are cheap.
  • Kid-friendly wow: Great Wall, Terracotta Warriors and a giant-panda base in one trip.
  • Easy internal travel: fast, comfortable bullet trains connect every city.

Health & vaccinations

No mandatory vaccinations for China. You do not need to present proof of any jab at the border. However, a visit to a travel health clinic 6–8 weeks before departure is strongly recommended for the whole family.

💉 Recommended vaccines

  • Hepatitis A — strongly advised for all travellers; transmitted through food and water, including street stalls.
  • Typhoid — recommended if you will be eating at local restaurants and street markets (you will).
  • Tetanus / diphtheria / polio — ensure the whole family's routine jabs are up to date before you travel.
  • Malaria: no risk in Beijing, Xi'an, Chengdu or Shanghai — no anti-malarial prophylaxis needed for this itinerary.

💊 Travel medical kit

  • Bring all regular medications (plus a 3-day surplus) and carry a doctor's letter in English explaining each prescription.
  • Pack: ibuprofen/paracetamol, oral rehydration salts, antihistamine, blister plasters, antiseptic wipes, and a thermometer.
  • Pharmacies (药店) are common in all four cities, but packaging is in Chinese and English-language products are rare — bring what your family uses.

🌫️ Air quality — Beijing & Xi'an

  • Both cities can have elevated particulate levels in autumn. Check AirVisual or AQICN.org daily and plan outdoor activities accordingly.
  • Pack KN95 / N95 masks for all four — use them on any high-AQI day; kids' lungs are more sensitive than adults'.
  • Chengdu and Shanghai are typically much cleaner; the concern is mainly the first few days in Beijing.

💧 Food & water safety

  • Never drink tap water — use bottled water even for brushing teeth. Bottled water is sold everywhere for pennies and is universally safe.
  • Hot drinks and high-temperature cooked food are safe. Busy street-food stalls with visible turnover are generally fine.
  • Avoid raw salads, unpeeled fruit and ice in drinks outside large hotel restaurants.

🏥 Travel insurance must-haves

  • Ensure your policy explicitly covers emergency medical evacuation — a medevac from China to Europe can exceed €50,000.
  • Save the insurer's 24/7 emergency number on every adult's phone before you board the outbound flight.
  • Keep all medical receipts in a dedicated envelope for any claims on return.

Getting around on the ground

✈️ Airport transfers

  • Beijing Capital (PEK): Airport Express train → Dongzhimen in ~25 min (≈€4/person). Skip taxis — they can take 60–90 min in traffic and cost €30–50.
  • Beijing Daxing (PKX): Daxing Airport Express → Caoqiao/Lixian in ~20–30 min. Check which airport your carrier uses before you fly — not all airlines use Capital.
  • Shanghai Pudong (PVG): The Maglev (431 km/h, 8 min to Longyang Rd, ≈€8) is an unforgettable experience for kids. Metro Line 2 is the budget alternative (50 min, ≈€1.50).
  • Shanghai Hongqiao (SHA): Metro Lines 2 & 10 — easy and very cheap.

🚇 City metros

  • All four cities have modern, clean, air-conditioned metro networks with English signage. They are the fastest and cheapest way around.
  • Use Alipay to buy metro QR codes, or get a UnionPay transit card to tap in and out. No paper tickets needed.
  • Passports are required for security checks at Beijing and Xi'an train stations — keep them on you (not in checked luggage).

🚄 Booking trains

  • Book bullet-train seats via Trip.com (formerly Ctrip) — English interface, accepts international Visa/Mastercard, delivers e-tickets to the app.
  • Collect physical tickets at the station if required: go to the self-service machines, insert passports, follow the English prompts.
  • Book Beijing–Xi'an and Xi'an–Chengdu legs 2–4 weeks in advance for October travel; these routes fill quickly.

🚕 Taxis & DiDi

  • Download DiDi (China's Uber) before arrival and link an international Visa/Mastercard. Drivers see the destination on-screen — no language barrier.
  • DiDi is reliable, metered, and cheap. Use it for airport-to-hotel runs in cities where the metro is inconvenient with luggage.
  • Street taxis are fine but always insist on the meter. Avoid touts at airports and major tourist entrances — they overcharge tourists.

📱 Essential apps — download before departure

  • DiDi — taxis & rideshare
  • Trip.com (Ctrip) — trains, flights & hotels
  • Alipay / WeChat Pay — payments everywhere
  • Baidu Maps or Gaode Maps — navigation (Google Maps is unreliable in China, even with a VPN)
  • Pleco — offline Chinese dictionary & camera translator
  • AirVisual — real-time air quality by city
  • Your chosen VPN — install and test it while still on European Wi-Fi before boarding

🧳 Luggage forwarding

  • Many mid-range and upmarket hotels offer city-to-city luggage forwarding (overnight delivery to your next hotel). Typically €10–15 per large bag. Ask at check-in.
  • Forwarding luggage ahead is a real quality-of-life upgrade when moving between four cities with kids — you travel light on the bullet train, bags arrive at the next hotel by morning.

Cultural tips & etiquette

🙏 General etiquette

  • Tipping is not expected — not in restaurants, taxis or hotels. Attempting to tip can cause genuine confusion or embarrassment.
  • Queuing is less strictly observed than in Europe; be assertive and patient at busy tourist site entrances and ticket windows.
  • Bargaining is expected at markets (Silk Market in Beijing, Yuyuan Bazaar in Shanghai). Start at 30–40% of the first asking price and be prepared to walk away. Never bargain in regular shops or supermarkets.
  • Loud conversation is normal in restaurants and public spaces — it's not considered rude.

🚽 Toilets

  • Squat toilets are common everywhere, including at some tourist sites. Always carry tissues and hand sanitiser — dispensers and paper dispensers are rare.
  • Major tourist sights (Great Wall Mutianyu, Terracotta Warriors, Panda Base) do have Western-style cubicles — look for the signage at the entrance.
  • Prepare a 9-year-old for the concept before the trip; practising once at home avoids stress on the road.

🍜 Dining etiquette

  • Do not stick chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice — it is associated with funeral rites. Rest them on the chopstick stand or across the rim of the bowl.
  • It is courteous to serve others before yourself when sharing dishes (the default style in Chinese restaurants).
  • Many restaurants use QR-code menus on the table — use Pleco or Google Translate's camera to read them.
  • Milk, cheese and dairy are rare in Chinese cuisine. If your kids depend on dairy, pack backup snacks from the hotel minimart.

📸 Photography rules

  • Photography is forbidden inside several halls of the Forbidden City and flash photography is prohibited at the Terracotta Warriors. Respect the posted signs — staff will intervene.
  • Avoid photographing military facilities, police officers, or government buildings that are not clearly tourist sites.
  • Chinese people — especially outside Beijing and Shanghai — may ask to take photos with fair-haired or Western-looking children. It is friendly curiosity; a polite smile and a gentle "no" is perfectly acceptable.

🗣️ Language & navigation

  • English is reasonably available at major tourist sites but rare in restaurants, shops, and smaller cities. Don't rely on it.
  • Save your hotel's address in Chinese characters on your phone (screenshot from the hotel's booking confirmation) — show it to taxi drivers and locals when lost.
  • Pleco's camera mode and Google Translate offline work well for reading menus and signs. Download Chinese language packs before you fly.

🚨 Emergency contacts

  • Police: 110  |  Ambulance: 120  |  Fire: 119
  • Latvian Embassy in Beijing: +86 10 5321 0001 — register your trip at iem.gov.lv before departure so the embassy can contact you in a crisis.
  • Keep a note of your travel insurer's emergency number, your nearest hospital address in each city, and a digital copy of all passports accessible offline.
Kids are a superpower in China. Chinese culture genuinely adores children — yours will receive warm smiles, occasional sweets, and enthusiastic attention everywhere you go. Prepare them for this: it's one of the most heartwarming parts of the experience.

What to take into consideration

🛂 Documents

  • All four passports valid 6+ months beyond travel, with a blank page.
  • Re-confirm visa-free terms before booking (embassy of China in Latvia; wife's Russia–China terms).
  • Carry printed hotel + return-flight confirmations.

💳 Money & payments

  • China is largely cashless — setting up Alipay and/or WeChat Pay for foreigners is increasingly supported but can be unreliable: not all European banks allow linking to these apps, and setup may fail at the verification step. Attempt the setup before you fly, test a small transaction, and do not rely on it as your only payment method.
  • Always carry a reserve of Chinese Yuan (¥) cash — essential for small vendors, street food stalls, rural sites, older taxis, and as a fallback if digital payments fail.
  • Exchange euros for Yuan at your home bank or at a Bank of China branch on arrival; airport exchange desks have poor rates. Avoid currency exchange kiosks at tourist sites.
  • Notify your bank of travel dates and destination before departure to prevent card blocks.
  • ATMs at Bank of China and ICBC reliably accept international Visa/Mastercard; aim for bank-branded ATMs rather than independent machines.

📱 Connectivity

  • Install a reputable VPN before arrival (ExpressVPN, NordVPN and Astrill all work well in China) — test it on European Wi-Fi before you board. VPNs cannot be downloaded once you are in China.
  • Apps blocked in China without a VPN: Google (Maps, Gmail, Chrome), WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube. Apps that work natively: WeChat, Baidu Maps, DiDi, Trip.com.
  • Buy a travel eSIM with a Chinese data plan (e.g. via Airalo or Klook) or activate roaming — data is needed for maps, translation and payments at all times.
  • Download offline maps (Maps.me or Baidu Maps offline packs) for each city before you arrive.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family practicalities

  • Book Great Wall (Mutianyu cable car + toboggan), Terracotta Warriors, Panda Base and key bullet trains at least 2–4 weeks ahead — October is busy.
  • Passports double as ID at train stations and many hotel check-ins — keep them on your person, not buried in luggage.
  • Pack layers + comfy, well-worn walking shoes. Expect 8–12 km of walking per full day.
  • Bring any regular medicines plus backups; don't count on finding your exact brand in China.
  • Clear the ~1 week of school absence with teachers in advance and ask for any key homework to take along.
  • A compact power bank is essential — phones drain fast navigating, translating and photographing all day.
  • Universal travel adapter: China uses Type A (flat two-pin) and Type I (angled) sockets; a multi-adapter covers both.
