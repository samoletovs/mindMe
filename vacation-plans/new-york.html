⚠️ Read this first — the visa hurdle

Your wife's Russian citizenship is the make-or-break issue. You and the kids (Latvian) just need a quick online ESTA. But your wife cannot use ESTA — she needs a full US B1/B2 visa with an in-person interview. Because she holds a Latvian residence permit she can apply in Riga, but first interview appointments commonly run several months out, plus possible "administrative processing." A confirmed visa before mid-October is possible but not guaranteed.
Decision rule: start her visa application now. If the visa is not confirmed by late September 2026, switch to the China plan rather than risk the whole trip.

Action steps if you choose New York

  1. Wife: complete DS-160 online carefully (every inconsistency can trigger administrative processing); pay fees (~$185 MRV fee + ~$250 EVUS/integrity fee ≈ $435+); and book the B1/B2 interview at the US Embassy in Riga (Raiņa bulvāris 7) immediately. Bring: DS-160 confirmation, passport, photo, fee receipt, employment letter, 3 months of bank statements, proof of property/ties, itinerary and return-flight intent.
  2. You + kids (Latvian): apply for ESTA online (~€21 each) no earlier than 3 years before travel and no later than 72 hours before departure. Valid for 2 years once approved.
  3. Prepare a strong supporting file for the interview: clear evidence that your wife has compelling reasons to return to Latvia (employment, children in Latvian schools, property).
  4. If the visa is denied: all fees are non-refundable. Do not book non-refundable travel until the visa stamp is in her passport. Denial also makes a future application harder — quality of the initial application matters.

Why New York & when to go

  • Great timing: October is one of the cheapest months on the Riga↔New York route, with pleasant cool-autumn weather.
  • Halloween bonus: you'd be in the US on 31 Oct — a huge highlight for a 9- and 12-year-old.
  • Easy for kids: English everywhere, world-class museums, and many free attractions.
  • Catch: lodging and food are far pricier than China — the main reason this plan costs more.

Weather late Oct: ~10–17 °C daytime, cool evenings — bring jackets and an umbrella.

How to keep New York cost-effective

🏨 Lodging

  • Stay in Queens or Jersey City, not Midtown — same subway access, much cheaper.
  • Book a family room/Airbnb with a kitchen to cook some meals.
  • Reserve early; October fills up around Halloween.

🍕 Food

  • Delis, halal carts, $1 pizza, Chinatown — eat like a local.
  • Pack breakfasts/snacks from grocery stores.

🚇 Getting around

  • 7-day Unlimited MetroCard (~$34/adult); young kids ride free. Skip taxis.
  • The Staten Island Ferry is free — great Liberty views.

🎟️ Attractions

  • Fill most days with free sights (parks, bridges, ferry, monuments).
  • Buy one CityPASS only if you'll do the big-ticket attractions.
  • Use pay-what-you-wish museum hours where available.

Where to stay — neighbourhood guide

Staying outside Manhattan cuts your lodging bill by 30–50% while keeping you 10–25 minutes from every major sight via subway or PATH train.

🏘️ Best-value areas for families

  • Astoria, Queens: Safe, diverse, lively food scene (Greek, Middle Eastern, international). N/W train to Midtown in 20–25 min. Hotels and rentals 30–40% cheaper than Manhattan. A top choice for families.
  • Long Island City, Queens: Directly across the river from Midtown with stunning skyline views; growing number of family-friendly hotels. E/M/7 subway to Midtown in 7 min — arguably the most convenient non-Manhattan base.
  • Jersey City (Downtown or Journal Square): PATH train to World Trade Center in ~10 min, running 24/7. Typically 40–50% cheaper than comparable Manhattan rooms. Free harbour views of the Manhattan skyline from Liberty State Park.
  • Brooklyn (Park Slope / Bay Ridge): Quiet, residential, excellent local restaurants, walkable to Prospect Park. 20–35 min to Midtown by subway. Very family-oriented atmosphere.

🏨 Booking tips

  • Book refundable rates until your wife's visa is confirmed — then lock in a cheaper non-refundable rate closer to October.
  • A self-catering apartment or Airbnb with a kitchen saves €20–40/day on family breakfasts and snacks; look for a unit with a laundry machine to travel lighter.
  • Late October (29–31 Oct) is in demand around Halloween — book those specific nights as early as possible.
  • NYC has strict short-term rental regulations since 2023: confirm the property is officially registered and hosts are allowed to rent it. Stick to established platforms and verified listings.
  • Check for an elevator if you'll have large suitcases — many NYC brownstones are walk-up buildings with no lift.

❌ Areas to avoid for family stays

  • Times Square / Midtown West: extremely noisy at all hours, and far more expensive for no practical benefit. Great to visit for an evening; not suited to sleeping.
  • East New York, Brownsville (Brooklyn), and South Bronx are statistically higher-crime residential areas — not recommended as tourist bases.

Getting there & getting around

✈️ JFK airport → your accommodation

  • AirTrain + Subway (recommended): AirTrain ($8.25) links all terminals to Jamaica Station. Take the E, J, or Z train into the city. Total: ~€13–15/person, ~55–70 min. With four people this saves €150–200 vs a taxi — worth it even with jet-lagged kids.
  • Rideshare (Uber / Lyft): Use the designated rideshare pick-up zone (signposted at arrivals). Expect €50–80 to Queens or Manhattan, plus surge pricing on busy evenings.
  • Yellow taxi: Fixed flat fare of $70 to Manhattan + tolls + tip ≈ €85+. No flat rate to other boroughs — meter runs.
  • Flying into Newark (EWR)? AirTrain to Newark Penn Station, then NJ Transit or Amtrak into NYC Penn Station (~35 min, ~€13/person).

🚇 Daily transit

  • NYC Subway: fastest and cheapest for any journey in the five boroughs. By October 2026 the entire network accepts OMNY contactless — tap your bank card or phone directly. A single ride is $2.90; the 7-day unlimited pass (~$34/person) pays off after 12 rides.
  • Children under 44 in (112 cm) ride free. Your 9-year-old may qualify; your 12-year-old will not — budget two adult passes.
  • PATH train (if in Jersey City): single fare ~$2.10, 24/7 service, very reliable.
  • Avoid yellow taxis for day-to-day sightseeing — they are slow in traffic and expensive. Subway is almost always faster.

🚶 Walking

  • Manhattan is built on a walkable grid. Many attractions are 15–30 min on foot. Expect to cover 8–12 km/day — prioritise broken-in, comfortable shoes over new ones.
  • Download Google Maps or Citymapper with NYC offline maps so you can navigate without mobile data in dead zones.

🚲 Citi Bike

  • NYC's bike-share has thousands of docking stations across Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens. A day pass is ~$15/person; excellent for exploring the waterfront, Central Park and the Brooklyn Bridge.
  • Minimum age to rent is 16; younger children cannot ride Citi Bikes independently but can join parents for casual cycling with their own bike or e-scooter rental.

📱 Useful apps

  • Google Maps — subway, bus and walking directions
  • Citymapper — very detailed real-time NYC transit routing
  • Uber / Lyft — rideshare for off-peak or luggage-heavy journeys
  • OpenTable / Resy — book popular restaurants 1–2 weeks ahead
  • NYC Go — official tourism app, free & discounted activity listings

Health & safety

US healthcare is the most expensive in the world. A single night in a US hospital can cost €5,000–15,000. Comprehensive family travel insurance with emergency medical evacuation cover is essential, not optional.

🏥 Medical emergencies

  • Emergency services: dial 911 (free from any phone). Non-emergency NYPD: 311.
  • For non-urgent illness (fever, minor injuries), use an Urgent Care clinic — faster than an ER and dramatically cheaper (€100–300/visit vs €1,000+ in an emergency room).
  • CVS and Walgreens pharmacies are on almost every block, many open 24/7, with comprehensive over-the-counter medicine.
  • Save your insurer's 24/7 claims line on every adult's phone before departure. Keep all medical receipts for reimbursement.

💊 Before you fly

  • No vaccinations are required for the United States. Ensure all routine jabs are current for the whole family.
  • Bring a full supply of any prescription medication — US pharmacies cannot dispense on a European prescription.
  • Pack children's medicines and sunscreen brands your family uses; US equivalents differ.

🔒 Personal safety

  • NYC is one of the safest large cities in the US. Tourist areas are very safe during daytime and early evenings.
  • Pickpocket hotspots: Times Square, busy subway platforms, crowded tourist queues. Use inner zip pockets or a money belt; never hang bags on chair backs.
  • Subway after midnight: safe on most lines, but choose busy, well-lit cars, avoid empty carriages, and stay aware of your surroundings.
  • Keep photocopies (or phone-camera scans) of all passports in a separate bag. Do not carry originals to tourist sites once you are settled in your accommodation.
  • Teach your kids your phone number and your accommodation address before any busy day out.

🌦️ Weather preparedness

  • Late October: ~10–17 °C but variable — a cold snap or heavy rain is entirely possible. Pack waterproof outer layers for all four.
  • Indoor spaces (museums, restaurants, subway cars) are heavily air-conditioned even in autumn — a light cardigan for everyone prevents chill indoors.

Cultural tips & practical know-how

💵 Tipping culture

  • Sit-down restaurants: 18–22% is the standard (on the pre-tax total). Many card readers prompt for 20%, 22% or 25% — 20% is the socially neutral choice.
  • Bars: $1–2 per drink ordered.
  • Taxis / Uber / Lyft: 15–20%, often pre-selected by the app.
  • Hotel housekeeping: $2–5/day, left in cash on the pillow (no digital option).
  • Counter service / fast food / food halls: tipping is optional; tip prompts appear everywhere but are not obligatory.
  • Budget tipping into your daily spend — it adds up noticeably for a family of four at restaurants.

🎃 Halloween — making the most of 31 Oct

  • Your flight home is on the 31st — schedule it for the evening and enjoy some Halloween festivities in the morning/afternoon.
  • The Greenwich Village Halloween Parade on 6th Avenue runs from ~7 pm; free to watch, with spectacular elaborate costumes — the city's premier Halloween event.
  • Neighbourhood trick-or-treating (Upper West Side, Park Slope in Brooklyn, Astoria in Queens) runs ~5–8 pm. A huge highlight for a 9- and 12-year-old.
  • Pick up inexpensive costumes at Party City or a dollar store a few days before; or pack lightweight ones from home.

🏷️ Prices, tax & portions

  • NYC sales tax is ~8.875%, added at checkout — prices on menus and shelves are pre-tax. Budget accordingly.
  • Clothing items under $110 are tax-free in New York State — useful for souvenir shopping.
  • American portions are large: sharing a main dish between the two kids often works perfectly and saves money.
  • Look for "prix fixe" lunch menus at restaurants you'd like to try for dinner — same kitchen, often 40% cheaper.

🗣️ Customs & practicalities

  • Americans are warm and helpful to tourists — never hesitate to ask for directions or recommendations.
  • Temperatures in °F, distances in miles: 60°F ≈ 16°C; 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km. A conversion app removes the mental arithmetic.
  • Jaywalking (crossing on red) is technically illegal but universally practised. Teach children to look carefully — traffic signal timing differs from what they know at home.
  • "Restroom" or "bathroom" are the standard terms for toilet; "WC" or "toilet" may draw a blank.
  • Shops typically open 10 am–9 pm; bodegas (corner stores) and delis often 24/7. No early Sunday closing as in Europe.

📶 Connectivity

  • Buy a US eSIM (T-Mobile, AT&T, or a travel eSIM via Airalo) for each adult's phone — excellent LTE/5G coverage throughout NYC and DC.
  • Free Wi-Fi is available at hotels, coffee shops, restaurants, and many subway stations. No VPN or special setup needed — all your regular apps work normally.

What to take into consideration

  • Visa timing is everything — do not book non-refundable flights or lodging until your wife's B1/B2 visa stamp is physically in her passport. "Processing" emails are not a visa.
  • Prepare a strong visa application file: 3 months of bank statements, employment letter, proof of property ties in Latvia, itinerary, hotel and return-flight confirmations, and family photos. A clear reason to return to Latvia (job, property, children in school) is the strongest argument for approval.
  • All four passports must be valid for at least 6 months beyond 1 Nov 2026 (i.e. valid until at least 1 May 2027) — check and renew now if needed.
  • Get comprehensive family travel + medical insurance — US healthcare is extraordinarily expensive; even a broken arm can cost €10,000+.
  • Book refundable lodging until the visa is confirmed; only then switch to cheaper non-refundable rates.
  • Clear the ~1 week of school absence with teachers in advance; ask for any important assignments the children can complete while travelling.
  • Carry a credit card with low or zero foreign-transaction fees (Revolut, Wise, or a travel card). Tipping (15–22%) is expected in restaurants — budget this into your daily food allowance.
  • Bring portable phone chargers for all-day city walking — subway navigation and map use drain batteries quickly.
  • US power sockets are 120V, Type A (flat two-pin). European appliances will need a voltage converter if they are not dual-voltage (check the label). Phone and laptop chargers are almost always 100–240V and only need a plug adapter.
