## TL;DR
Spaced repetition is a learning technique where I review information at increasing intervals to help it stick in long-term memory. It's most effective for facts, vocabulary, and skills I want to retain for the long haul, and is the backbone of tools like Anki and SuperMemo.

## Body
Spaced repetition leverages the spacing effect: the idea that information is better remembered when study sessions are spaced out over time, rather than crammed together. The method was first described by Hermann Ebbinghaus in the 1880s, who also developed the concept of the forgetting curve. Since then, spaced repetition has been validated in many studies and is widely used in language learning, medical education, and skill acquisition.

Popular systems include the Leitner system (a manual flashcard method), SuperMemo (software with algorithmic scheduling), and Anki (customizable flashcard software). The core principle is to review material just as I'm about to forget it, which strengthens memory and reduces wasted effort.
