## TL;DR
Spaced repetition is a learning technique that uses increasing intervals between reviews to help information stick in long-term memory. It's most often used with flashcards and is backed by strong research, especially for things like vocabulary or facts. The method dates back to Ebbinghaus in the 1880s and has evolved into various systems and software like Anki and SuperMemo. The core idea is that reviewing material just as you're about to forget it is much more effective than cramming.

## Highlights
- Spaced repetition leverages the spacing effect—reviewing information at expanding intervals boosts retention.
- It's especially effective for vocabulary, facts, and skills that need to be remembered long-term.
- The Leitner system, SuperMemo, and Anki are popular implementations, each with their own algorithms for scheduling reviews.
- Research consistently shows spaced repetition outperforms massed practice (cramming) for durable learning.
- "Acts of revision should be spaced in gradually increasing intervals, roughly intervals of one day, two days, four days, eight days, and so on." — C. A. Mace (1932)
- "Spaced repetition is widely accepted as a performant learning strategy in a number of domains, with many researchers suggesting implementing this method in formal education."
